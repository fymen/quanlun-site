/An Unconventional Treatise on Martial Arts: Reflections on the Internal Martial Arts/, by Feng Mao.

This book is for readers with some knowledge of the internal martial arts and practical experience who want a deeper understanding of their principles. It focuses on principles and methods of cultivation. Specific forms and movements should be studied with an in-person teacher or relevant martial manuals.

** Where to begin
Begin with [[/en/read/kongjing/][“Emptiness and Stillness”]] to explore the author’s explanation of these terms. Continue with [[/en/read/breath_gap/][“When Inhalation and Exhalation Contain Each Other”]], then read [[/en/read/piquan_quanjia/][“To Become an Immortal, Practise Splitting Fist”]]. This takes you from emptiness and stillness in wuji, through breathing, to Sun-style splitting fist.

** The book’s seven parts
*** 1. Xingyiquan
Wuji posture, the three-body posture, splitting fist, and other practices provide a route into the principles of movement, alignment, and awareness.

[[/en/read/piquan_quanjia/][Read “To Become an Immortal, Practise Splitting Fist” →]]

*** 2. Understanding internal power
Exploring internal power from different angles, with the author’s observations and practical experience.

*** 3. Methods of the Sun tradition
The methods of the Sun tradition, discussed through teachings received from teachers and the writings of earlier masters.

*** 4. The way of breathing
Breathing and its relationship to practice, with attention to the context behind different descriptions.

[[/en/read/breath_gap/][Read “When Inhalation and Exhalation Contain Each Other” →]]

*** 5. Stages of martial practice
The author’s reflections on understanding the arts and developing through practice.

*** 6. Stories from the martial world
Stories and encounters, and what they can teach us about practice.

*** 7. Practical questions
Observations, questions, and insights arising from specific problems in practice.

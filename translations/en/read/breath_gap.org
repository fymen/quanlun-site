Ordinary people breathe with their lungs. This breathing has no power; it is also called external breathing.

Martial artists place greater importance on another system of breathing: dantian breathing, also called internal breathing.

This is not limited to martial arts. Singing, dancing, swimming, and other activities, once practised beyond a superficial level, all involve internal breathing. When internal and external breathing can come together, this is the unity of inside and outside.

The old masters said that inhaling should lift a person and exhaling should send a person out. They were referring to breathing in which the internal and external have become one.

At this point, both inhaling and exhaling carry considerable power. It is the power of centeredness and harmony: a penetrating force that cannot be opposed with brute strength.

You can no longer strike such a person during inhalation or exhalation. Instead, you must strike in the gap between them. This is called “striking the breathing gap”.

For someone whose skill is higher still, even this gap disappears. This is when inhalation and exhalation contain each other.

In an ordinary person, the chest and back expand on inhalation and contract on exhalation. Between the two is a pause: the breathing gap.

A skilled practitioner, however, can expand the chest and back on inhalation and continue expanding them on exhalation, with no gap between. This is also the key to the opening-and-closing hands in Sun-style Taijiquan: opening is inhaling and expanding the back; closing is exhaling and continuing to expand the back.

“Inhalation and exhalation contain each other” means that the body’s shape during inhalation and exhalation is almost the same. Strictly speaking, though, they are not identical. The earlier masters’ choice of the word “contain” is particularly apt.

In the phrase “qi rests and the pulse is still”, /qi/ refers to ordinary chest breathing, while /pulse/ refers to the ordinary pattern of the qi channels—that is, the circulation of qi and blood.

“Qi rests” means that, when inhalation and exhalation contain each other, the person’s former way of breathing ceases to operate and is replaced by this new way. It does not mean, as repeated misunderstandings suggest, that the person no longer breathes.

“The pulse is still” means that, in this state, the former pattern of qi and blood circulation is taken over by the pattern associated with inhalation and exhalation containing each other. It does not mean entering dormancy.

“Inhalation and exhalation contain each other” and “qi rests and the pulse is still” describe the same thing from two different angles.

What Taijiquan practitioners often call “opening and closing residing within each other” is this state of inhalation and exhalation containing each other, with qi resting and the pulse still.
